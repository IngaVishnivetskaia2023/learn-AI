## AI SDLC

### SME (Subject Matter Expert)

#### Discovery
The phase where work first enters the SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle). It is where the problem is framed before solution design begins.

#### SDLC
The structured process used to plan, build, test, deploy, and maintain software.

#### Entry Framing
How a problem or request is defined at the start of discovery. Poor framing can distort every later stage of the project.

#### Downstream Stage
Any later phase in the process that depends on the quality of discovery inputs, such as design, development, testing, or delivery.

#### AI Explores Problems
AI can help surface patterns, options, and candidate problem statements, but it should not be the final decision-maker.

#### Human Decision-Making
The role of people in selecting which problem is worth solving, based on judgment, strategy, and context.

#### L3 Consulting / SME Superpower
The ability to create shared reusable context — a common understanding that can be reused across teams, stages, and decisions.

#### Shared Reusable Context
Information, assumptions, and reasoning that are documented so others can use them later without re-discovering them.

#### Continuous Discovery
An ongoing process of learning from production, users, and business signals to keep the opportunity backlog updated.

#### Opportunity Backlog
A list of possible problems, needs, or improvements that may be worth pursuing.

#### Production Signal
Real-world feedback from the live product or environment, such as usage data, incidents, support tickets, or user behavior.

#### Wide Risk Gates
Four key checks used to evaluate an opportunity:
- Value
- Usability
- Feasibility
- Viability

#### Value
Whether the opportunity solves a real business or user need.

#### Usability
Whether people can effectively use the proposed solution.

#### Feasibility
Whether the solution can realistically be built with the available technology, time, and skills.

#### Viability
Whether the solution makes sense for the business model, cost, and long-term sustainability.

#### Decision Memory
A documented record of why a decision was made, especially when consensus turns into an actionable choice.

#### Consensus
Agreement among stakeholders. It becomes more valuable when captured as Decision Memory.

#### Repo Artefact
A documented item stored in the repository, such as a brief, decision note, research summary, or opportunity framing.

#### Named Downstream Stage
A specific later stage in the process that is expected to consume the framed opportunity, such as design, planning, or implementation.

#### Eval-Shaped Metrics
Metrics that measure value or quality of outcomes, often tied to evaluation criteria.

#### Adoption-Shaped Metrics
Metrics that measure usage or uptake, such as engagement, activation, or retention.

#### Vertical SMEs
Subject matter experts focused on a specific **domain, product area, or business function**. They often notice domain-specific failure modes.

#### Horizontal SMEs
Experts focused on cross-cutting practices or systems, such as **design, architecture, operations, or governance**. They often spot systemic failure modes.

#### Failure Modes
The ways a process, solution, or assumption can break down.

#### Responsible AI
A way of designing and using AI that considers safety, fairness, transparency, accountability, and human oversight.

#### Discovery-First Responsible AI
The idea that responsible AI should begin before build phase — during discovery, where risks and impacts are first identified.

#### Business Lens
A viewpoint that asks whether an opportunity supports business goals, strategy, cost, and value.

#### Technology Lens
A viewpoint that asks whether the opportunity is technically possible, maintainable, and scalable.

#### Commodity
Something standard or widely available that usually should be bought or consumed instead of built from scratch.

#### Novel
Something new, unique, or differentiated that may justify building custom solutions.

#### Build / Buy / Consume
A decision framework for choosing whether to create something internally, purchase it, or use an existing service.

#### Weak Brief
A poorly written or incomplete request that hides assumptions, lacks clarity, or creates confusion.

#### Visible Tells
Clues in a weak brief that reveal what is missing or broken.

#### Broken Principle
The underlying discovery principle that a weak brief violates, such as unclear value, missing users, or unrealistic feasibility.

#### Push-Back Question
A clarifying challenge question used to test assumptions and improve the framing of the problem.

Value, usability, feasibility, viability are the four main gates.
Decisions should be documented as memory.
Responsible AI begins in discovery.


## Workflows the role does:

#### Engagement kickoff (старт проекта)
— receive client goals, regulatory context, prior learnings; agree on scope and the decisions to be made.

#### Idea capture 
— collect and document new product ideas, opportunities, or improvement suggestions from internal sources (sales conversations, support tickets, production signal, executive prompts, team retrospectives) and external sources (market feeds, analyst notes, customer interviews) into a structured idea backlog. Deduplicate against existing entries on intake.
Всё складывается в структурированный бэклог идей. При добавлении — проверка на дубли с тем, что уже есть.

#### Current-state assessment 
— document the function as it runs today: as-is process, volume, FTEs, and baseline KPIs, with each pain point and gap tied to a named KPI deficit (which KPI sits below benchmark, by how much, costing how much per quarter). Produces the baseline the opportunity brief's value and ROI hypotheses are measured against.
FTEs:	Full-Time Equivalents — сколько людей задействовано
Baseline KPIs:	Текущие показатели (время обработки, ошибки, стоимость)

#### Discovery cycle 
— multi-source research → synthesis → opportunity framing → risk-gate evaluation → recommendation.

#### Opportunity assessment 
— evaluate one or more candidate opportunities against the four (extended to six for AI-native) risk gates and produce a recommendation.
| Gate | Вопрос |
| :-- | :-- |
| Value | Нужно ли пользователю? |
| Usability | Смогут ли пользоваться? |
| Feasibility | Можем ли построить? |
| Viability | Выживет ли как бизнес? |

#### Handoff to PM/BA 
— transfer the opportunity brief, context pack, and Decision Memory; clarify open questions; agree on what re-opens the brief.

| Что передаёт | Что это |
| :-- | :-- |
| Opportunity brief | Бриф возможности (value + ROI гипотезы) |
| Context pack | Пакет контекста (всё что нужно знать) |
| Decision Memory | Память решений (что решили и почему) |


#### Delivery advisory 
— ongoing input through delivery on long engagements: monitor production signal and eval drift, surface scope-drift candidates, advise on go/no-go at stage gates, refresh the opportunity brief and Decision Memory when learning lands.

#### Outcome feedback 
— once functionality ships, review what was built against the opportunity brief and provide structured feedback: does the implemented behavior match the value hypothesis, do measured outcomes (KPIs, eval scores, adoption signal) track the ROI hypothesis, and which gaps need a brief amendment, a context-pack update, or a new Decision Memory entry. The loop closes back into Continuous Discovery.

#### Continuous Discovery 
— on long engagements, the discovery cycle becomes an ongoing rhythm: weekly customer-contact, monthly opportunity-backlog review, quarterly context-pack refresh, plus on-trigger reviews fired by market, regulatory, or production signal.

#### Cross-engagement reuse 
— at engagement end, extract reusable assets (horizontal context-pack additions, prompt-library entries, eval-rubric refinements) into firm-level storage.

| Актив | Пример |
| :-- | :-- |
| Horizontal context-pack additions | Общие для индустрии контекстные файлы |
| Prompt-library entries | Рабочие промпт-шаблоны |
| Eval-rubric refinements | Улучшенные критерии оценки |

## A good mental model
🐦 Before: “Ask the SME what they know.”
🐉 At L3: “Help the SME turn what they know into a reusable artefact.”
That is what **SME-as-platform** means in practice.

#### New artefacts the role produces: 
- A native-format role-agent (a Claude Skill or sub-agent) — the L3 cut on "the SME's approach, written down and runnable." When the SME is unavailable, the agent runs the discovery loop in their style.
- A version-controlled context pack with named owner, refresh cadence, and retirement criteria.
- A Decision Memory corpus the team consults (not a write-only artefact).
- Eval rubrics for the briefs themselves — the L3 firm grades its own briefs the same way it grades the AI capabilities the briefs propose.


## Vertical and Horizontal SME

A banking SME may catch the edge case hidden inside a good metric. The model may show 97% accuracy, but the missed 3% are the cases a regulator audits. The first production miss can turn a metric win into a compliance incident.
A finance SME may catch a reusable control pattern that appears across industries.
A strong brief usually consults both. 
A **vertical-only brief** may be domain-accurate but technically unsafe. 
A **horizontal-only brief** may be technically clean and aimed at the wrong problem.

### The SME becomes a platform
The role is shifting.
- The old SME was often "the person who knows." The L3 SME becomes the source of reusable context that agents and teams can use.
Useful SME knowledge now appears as:
- an industry context pack;
- annotated examples that calibrate model output;
- decision rules for recurring edge cases;
- a role-agent spec that runs the discovery loop in the SME's style.

The SME keeps authority. The knowledge also works when the SME is away.

## Keep knowledge in artefacts
The test is simple:
A teammate can recover the context from the repo.

#### Strong version:
- opportunity-brief.md exists;
- the context pack exists;
- Decision Memory entries exist;
- downstream roles can link to them.

#### Weak version:
- the team ran a workshop;
- people remember the context;
- the deck is the only source;
- a teammate needs a meeting to recover the reasoning.

The deck can be a view. The repo artefacts are the source of truth.
