## AI SDLC

### SME (Subject Matter Expert)

#### Discovery
The phase where work first enters the SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle). It is where the problem is framed before solution design begins.

#### SDLC
The structured process used to plan, build, test, deploy, and maintain software.

#### Entry Framing
How a problem or request is defined at the start of discovery. Poor framing can distort every later stage of the project.

#### Downstream Stage
Any later phase in the process that depends on the quality of discovery inputs, such as design, development, testing, or delivery.

#### AI Explores Problems
AI can help surface patterns, options, and candidate problem statements, but it should not be the final decision-maker.

#### Human Decision-Making
The role of people in selecting which problem is worth solving, based on judgment, strategy, and context.

#### L3 Consulting / SME Superpower
The ability to create shared reusable context — a common understanding that can be reused across teams, stages, and decisions.

#### Shared Reusable Context
Information, assumptions, and reasoning that are documented so others can use them later without re-discovering them.

#### Continuous Discovery
An ongoing process of learning from production, users, and business signals to keep the opportunity backlog updated.

#### Opportunity Backlog
A list of possible problems, needs, or improvements that may be worth pursuing.

#### Production Signal
Real-world feedback from the live product or environment, such as usage data, incidents, support tickets, or user behavior.

#### Wide Risk Gates
Four key checks used to evaluate an opportunity:
- Value
- Usability
- Feasibility
- Viability

#### Value
Whether the opportunity solves a real business or user need.

#### Usability
Whether people can effectively use the proposed solution.

#### Feasibility
Whether the solution can realistically be built with the available technology, time, and skills.

#### Viability
Whether the solution makes sense for the business model, cost, and long-term sustainability.

#### Decision Memory
A documented record of why a decision was made, especially when consensus turns into an actionable choice.

#### Consensus
Agreement among stakeholders. It becomes more valuable when captured as Decision Memory.

#### Repo Artefact
A documented item stored in the repository, such as a brief, decision note, research summary, or opportunity framing.

#### Named Downstream Stage
A specific later stage in the process that is expected to consume the framed opportunity, such as design, planning, or implementation.

#### Eval-Shaped Metrics
Metrics that measure value or quality of outcomes, often tied to evaluation criteria.

#### Adoption-Shaped Metrics
Metrics that measure usage or uptake, such as engagement, activation, or retention.

#### Vertical SMEs
Subject matter experts focused on a specific **domain, product area, or business function**. They often notice domain-specific failure modes.

#### Horizontal SMEs
Experts focused on cross-cutting practices or systems, such as **design, architecture, operations, or governance**. They often spot systemic failure modes.

#### Failure Modes
The ways a process, solution, or assumption can break down.

#### Responsible AI
A way of designing and using AI that considers safety, fairness, transparency, accountability, and human oversight.

#### Discovery-First Responsible AI
The idea that responsible AI should begin before build phase — during discovery, where risks and impacts are first identified.

#### Business Lens
A viewpoint that asks whether an opportunity supports business goals, strategy, cost, and value.

#### Technology Lens
A viewpoint that asks whether the opportunity is technically possible, maintainable, and scalable.

#### Commodity
Something standard or widely available that usually should be bought or consumed instead of built from scratch.

#### Novel
Something new, unique, or differentiated that may justify building custom solutions.

#### Build / Buy / Consume
A decision framework for choosing whether to create something internally, purchase it, or use an existing service.

#### Weak Brief
A poorly written or incomplete request that hides assumptions, lacks clarity, or creates confusion.

#### Visible Tells
Clues in a weak brief that reveal what is missing or broken.

#### Broken Principle
The underlying discovery principle that a weak brief violates, such as unclear value, missing users, or unrealistic feasibility.

#### Push-Back Question
A clarifying challenge question used to test assumptions and improve the framing of the problem.

Value, usability, feasibility, viability are the four main gates.
Decisions should be documented as memory.
Responsible AI begins in discovery.
